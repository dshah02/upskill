Abstract

Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has improved the reasoning abilities of large language models (LLMs) on mathematics and programming tasks, often by maximizing pass@1 correctness. However, optimizing single-attempt accuracy can inadvertently suppress response diversity across repeated attempts, narrowing exploration and overlooking underrepresented strategies. We introduce UpSkill, a training time method that adapts Mutual Information Skill Learning (MISL) to LLMs to induce structured response diversity: a discrete latent $z$ selects a reproducible "strategy" that steers the token distribution toward distinct modes. We propose a novel reward that we implement within Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO): a token-level mutual information (MI) reward that encourages trajectory specificity to $z$. Experiments on GSM8K with three open-weight models—Llama 3.1–8B, Qwen 2.5–7B, and R1-Distilled–Qwen2.5–Math–1.5B—show that UpSkill improves multi-attempt metrics for Qwen 2.5–7B, yielding gains of ~4% in pass@k and ~4% in plurality@k without degrading pass@1. Additionally, we prove that improvements in pass@k are closely tied to the mutual information objective, providing a theoretical justification for UpSkill.

The UpSkill Method

1. The Problem: Response Collapse

Standard RLVR training with pass@1 objectives can lead to response collapse, where models produce highly similar outputs across multiple attempts. This reduces the effective number of independent attempts and limits pass@k performance. UpSkill addresses this by explicitly training for diversity while maintaining correctness.

2. Mutual Information Objective

We maximize the mutual information $\mathcal{I}(\tau; z \mid x)$ between a discrete latent strategy $z \in \{1, \ldots, N\}$ and trajectories $\tau$. This encourages: (i) high marginal entropy $H(\tau \mid x)$ for broad coverage, and (ii) low conditional entropy $H(\tau \mid x, z)$ so each $z$ induces a reproducible mode.

3. Token-Level MI Reward

We implement the MI objective through a token-level reward:

$$r_{\text{TMI}}(\tau_i; x, z) = \sum_{t=1}^{|\tau|} \left[\log p_\pi(y_t \;|\; x, z, y_{\lt t}) - \log p_\pi(y_t \;|\; x, y_{\lt t})\right]$$

where the second term is a uniform mixture over all strategies. This reward is combined with correctness and KL penalties in GRPO training.

4. Theoretical Guarantee

We prove that pass@k improvement is bounded by mutual information:

$$1 - \exp\left(-C_1\eta^2\mathcal{I}(\tau; z \mid x)^2\right) \leq \frac{\text{pass@k}_M - \text{pass@k}_B}{1 - \text{pass@k}_B}$$

This shows that maximizing MI provably improves multi-attempt success rates, providing theoretical justification for our approach.

Key Findings

  • UpSkill achieves +3.6% in pass@k and +4.0% in plurality@k on GSM8K for Qwen 2.5–7B without degrading pass@1 accuracy.
  • Token-level mutual information reward successfully prevents response collapse during RLVR training while maintaining correctness.
  • In controlled arithmetic environments, UpSkill improves pass@5 by +10% by developing diverse and complementary reasoning strategies.
  • Theoretical analysis proves pass@k improvement is monotonically related to mutual information $\mathcal{I}(\tau; z \mid x)$, justifying the MI objective.
  • Different latent strategies $z$ produce semantically distinct, reproducible reasoning approaches rather than superficial formatting differences.
  • The method provides a training-time solution to diversity that doesn't require prompt engineering or inference-time modifications.
